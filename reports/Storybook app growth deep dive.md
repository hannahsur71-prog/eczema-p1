# Paid ads built Storybook, faith content outgrew it

Storybook grew mainly by buying parents through Facebook and Instagram ads, not through word of mouth. Over two thirds of customers came from paid Meta ads in 2020–21 ([Alts.co](https://alts.co/should-you-invest-in-storybook-a-sleep-app-for-parents/)). Those ads fed a long personalization quiz, a soft paywall and a 7-day trial that converted to an annual plan, and the founders then raised that annual price by 50–85%. Familify Corp, run by Ecuadorian husband-and-wife founders Francisco Cornejo and Daniela Vega, began with one founder-story mechanic: Vega's infant massage combined with narrated bedtime stories, so the phone could stay face down. A claimed viral post and Spanish-language press coverage carried the app across Latin America. Downloads then rose from 1.5M (2021) to a claimed 4M+. Revenue stalled at roughly **$0.8–0.9M a year in 2022–23**, far short of the founders' 2022 forecast of more than $2M. Cornejo later described Storybook as "well reviewed but wasn't growing" ([TWiST 2214](https://www.thisweekinstartups.com/2214)). Bible stories tested inside Storybook outperformed, so in early 2025 the team spun them out as **Theo: Prayer & Meditation**. Within weeks Theo's daily sales passed Storybook's, and most of the team now works on Theo. Users give Storybook about 4.8 stars on iOS but 3.9 on Google Play, and the negative reviews focus on surprise annual charges and cancellation that does not work. For a screen-free, eczema-focused moisturizer-massage-and-story app, three things are worth copying: the parent-guided touch ritual, the founder-expert story, and the quiz-funnel mechanics. Three weaknesses are open to exploit: Storybook's generic "sleep" positioning, its billing reputation, and the absence of any organic community.

## A massage class and a claimed viral post seeded a 2018 launch

The idea came from a specific gap between two bedtime tasks. The couple moved from Ecuador to Australia in early 2015 with sons aged 1 and 3. Vega began studying infant massage there and found she could not massage and read a story at the same time ([El País Bolivia](https://elpais.bo/curiosidades/20190516_masajes-y-cuentos-a-la-vez-para-dormir-a-los-ninos-como-es-la-app-que-se-populariza-entre-los-padres.html)). Her "aha moment" came when her massage instructor asked how she connects with her kids ([Florida Trend](https://www.floridatrend.com/article/34022/husband-and-wife-team-launch-storybook-app-to-help-parents-connect-with-kids/)). Cornejo calls it "100% her idea." Vega wrote all the story content and voices the Spanish stories ([StartUp Beat](https://startupbeat.com/this-company-wants-to-redefine-the-parent-child-interaction/35723/); [Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/noticias/tecnologia/storybook-app-ecuatoriana-8-millones/)). Cornejo brought distribution skills. He had sold three businesses by 25, served as a former Honda LatAm CMO, and holds an RMIT communications master's ([Cornejo author page](https://www.storybook-app.com/en/author/francisco-cornejo)). Press material describes Vega as an accredited child therapist "under the support of the Massage Association of Australia," which is a company claim ([EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/553705841/storybook-app-helps-children-and-parents-sleep-better-without-screen-time)).

The viral-launch story rests on a single source, a company-distributed release. It says Vega "shared her story on social media, and it went viral with **over 1 million views and 33000 comments**. This viral story was used to launch the MVP" ([OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/2250347/storybook-here-s-an-app-that-s-changing-the-lives-of-parents)). The Spanish About page goes further, claiming her techniques drew **10M+ views**, and mentions a Facebook moms group of 12,000+ members ([Storybook ES](https://www.storybook-app.com/es/sobre-nosotros)). No source names the platform, the date or the post itself. Facebook is the most likely venue, but that is inference. The app was first published under an Australian entity, Sailor Bear Pty Ltd ([AppGrooves](https://appgrooves.com/app/storybook-infant-massage-and-bed-time-stories-by-sailor-bear-pty-ltd)). The App Store ID (id1355190474) points to early 2018, consistent with a Q1 2018 launch ([El País Bolivia](https://elpais.bo/curiosidades/20190516_masajes-y-cuentos-a-la-vez-para-dormir-a-los-ninos-como-es-la-app-que-se-populariza-entre-los-padres.html)). Florida Trend's "2019" most likely marks the wider public or US launch. The Miami Herald reports that **300 families paid on launch day** ([Miami Herald via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/miami-herald-sunday/20220508/281874416991544)). By May 2019 the company claimed **#1 in Google Play's Parenting category in 60+ countries** ([El País Bolivia](https://elpais.bo/curiosidades/20190516_masajes-y-cuentos-a-la-vez-para-dormir-a-los-ninos-como-es-la-app-que-se-populariza-entre-los-padres.html)). That kind of rank is cheap to win in small storefronts, but it gave the company a durable marketing line that grew over time to "90+," "150+" and "160" countries.

## Two thirds of customers came from paid Meta ads

The founder said it directly in November 2021: the company "relied mainly on paid acquisition on Facebook and Instagram" and was "now seeing potential in partnerships and organic growth, which takes a long time to build" ([GetLatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/storybook-app#team)). Alts.co reviewed the Republic raise and put **paid FB/IG ads at more than two thirds of customers**. It also flagged weak App Store discoverability and a large pool of free downloads that never convert ([Alts.co](https://alts.co/should-you-invest-in-storybook-a-sleep-app-for-parents/)). The only creative recovered is a US TikTok ad from November 2021. It used a social-proof hook, "used by more than 20,000 mothers in the US." It drew 89,528 views over 23 days on an estimated **$149–$388** of spend, so TikTok looks like a test channel rather than a core one ([Pipiads](https://pipiads.com/tiktok-ads-examples/Storybook:Sleep&MassageApp-tiktok-ads-1717531469047842)). No source discloses CPI, CAC, ROAS or total spend. The single unit-economics figure is a relayed **3:1 LTV:CAC** alongside a claim of "surpassed $1M ARR" ([Ganas / Lolita Taub](https://lolitataub.substack.com/p/special-edition-ganas-and-i-invested)).

Organic channels were secondary. The company built an SEO blog on storybook-app.com. It publishes listicles that rank its own app ("The 10 Best Apps to Help Your Kids Fall Asleep") and "free massage" lead magnets ([storybook-app.com](https://www.storybook-app.com/en/sleep/sleep-app-for-kids)). It later posted a Community Builder role focused on Pinterest and Instagram ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/storybook-app)). No influencer or mom-blogger campaigns surfaced, and follower counts could not be verified.

The listing moved into Health & Fitness, and its target widened from infants to ages 0–12. The store copy claims 400+ tracks and 100+ massage techniques with weekly drops ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.storybook.story&hl=en_US)). Apple ran an "Apps We Love" editorial story on the founders, probably around 2024 based on its story ID, and Familify claims an Apple "Best for Bedtime" recognition ([App Store story](https://apps.apple.com/bf/ipad/story/id1736010445); [familify.com](https://familify.com/)). Storybook asks for a review right after a parent rates a story highly ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/storybook-sleep-meditation)). That probably helps explain why iOS sits at about **4.8 from 3.4–4.7k ratings** while Google Play sits at **3.9 from about 3.7k** ([Selfpause](https://www.selfpause.com/resources/storybook); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.storybook.story&hl=en_US)).

### A stack of trust badges, nearly all self-reported

Storybook layers credibility signals. It names the Family Sleep Institute and Infant Massage USA in its listings and claims a kidSAFE Seal and a Mom's Choice Award. It holds a NAPPA "Best for Baby" (2021) and joined the Inspired Internet Pledge in November 2024 ([NAPPA](https://www.nappaawards.com/product/storybook/); [Inspired Internet](https://inspiredinternet.org/signatory/storybook-app/); [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1355190474/storybook-sleep-massage-app)). Its outcome numbers, "80% of children sleep better" and "92% of users report their bedtime routine improved," come from a "scientific committee" and internal surveys with no published methodology ([EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/553705841/storybook-app-helps-children-and-parents-sleep-better-without-screen-time); [get.storybook-app.com](https://get.storybook-app.com/en/)). A 2022 announcement of a clinical trial with Florida International University has no published results that this research could find ([Florida Trend](https://www.floridatrend.com/article/34022/husband-and-wife-team-launch-storybook-app-to-help-parents-connect-with-kids/)). Only the NAPPA listing and the pledge page are hosted independently.

### Spanish-language earned media did the heavy lifting in LatAm

PR came in two streams. Paid wire releases (EIN Presswire, openPR) pushed "the world's only software app to combine infant massage techniques and bedtime storytelling" and the screen-free angle. Earned founder-story coverage came mainly from Spanish-language business press. The peak was Forbes Colombia's January 2021 headline, "Creó una app de masajes y cuentos para niños, ahora está valuada en US$8 millones," tied to an $800K Calacanis-led round ([Forbes Colombia](https://forbes.co/2021/01/14/actualidad/creo-una-app-de-masajes-y-cuentos-para-ninos-ahora-esta-valuada-en-us8-millones/)). Time Out México, La República, NTN24 and Ecuadorian outlets followed, and Miami Herald and Florida Trend coverage came in 2022 ([Time Out MX](https://www.timeoutmexico.mx/ciudad-de-mexico/ninyos/storybook-la-app-de-masajes-y-cuentos-para-que-tus-ninyos-duerman-mejor); [NTN24](https://www.ntn24.com/noticias-ciencia-y-tecnologia/storybook-mejora-el-sueno-de-bebes-y-ninos-397698)). The app ships in Spanish, English and Portuguese, with Arabic and Japanese listed later. Its purchasing-power pricing ranges from about $36/yr (early LatAm) to $39.99 in Tunisia and $59.99 in the US ([Primicias](https://www.primicias.ec/noticias/tecnologia/storybook-app-ecuatoriana-8-millones/); [App Store TN](https://apps.apple.com/tn/app/storybook-calm-bedtime-sleep/id1355190474)). The company moved its headquarters to Miami to chase "tens of millions of families," and Cornejo said "Miami is the perfect place for us" ([Refresh Miami](https://refreshmiami.com/news/storybook-scores-700000-in-funding-during-miami-tech-week/)).

## A 30-step quiz, a soft paywall and steep price increases drove monetization

The funnel is a standard quiz-to-trial subscription design. ScreensDesign's teardown counts **30 onboarding steps** framed as a personalized "consultation." The quiz asks for the child's name and age, ADHD or neurodivergence, the child's "love language," and whether the child sleeps in their own room. A **soft, dismissible paywall** offering a 7-day free trial comes only after this investment, and the reviewer flags the quiz length as friction ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/storybook-sleep-meditation); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/storybook-sleep-meditation/id1355190474)). The web special-offer page promises a reminder on day 5 ([specialoffer](https://www.storybook-app.com/en/specialoffer)). The terms require cancelling at least 24 hours before the trial ends, and they refuse refunds except for a faulty app within 30 days ([User Agreement](https://www.storybook-app.com/user-agreement)).

Pricing is where the company pushed hardest. In-app listings show a **$14.99 monthly plan used as an anchor** next to yearly SKUs at $47.99, $49.99, $59.99 and $89.99 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/storybook-sleep-meditation/id1355190474)). During the Republic raise the founders wrote: "After A/B testing different price points we have found an opportunity to increase the yearly price to 89.99 USD." They said increases of **50–85%** came "without a significant affection to the conversion rates" in most countries ([Alts.co](https://alts.co/should-you-invest-in-storybook-a-sleep-app-for-parents/)). One user traced the climb from $46 to $56 to "now $90" ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1355190474/storybook-sleep-massage-app/contact)). At the same time, StackSocial and its syndicated stores (Entrepreneur, Android Authority) sold a year of Premium for **$29.99 against an "$89.99 value"**, and Krazy Coupon Lady promoted "over 65% off" around Black Friday 2021 ([StackSocial](https://www.stacksocial.com/sales/1-year-of-storybook-premium-baby-sleep-app-for-better-bedtime-for-only-29); [Krazy Coupon Lady](https://thekrazycouponlady.com/2021/12/01/storybook-parenting-app-1-year-for-over-65-off)). The high list price made the discount channels look like bargains.

Retention depends on habit and annual billing more than on engagement features. Users build a personalized nightly routine of stories, massage, meditation and affirmations in a Routine menu, and new content is claimed weekly ([appexploring](https://www.appexploring.com/app/storybook-calm-bedtime-sleep/)). The research found **no streaks, no confirmed Family Sharing and no published D1/D30 or renewal data**. Some users call the content "subpar and way too short" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.storybook.story&hl=en_US)). A 2026 roundup notes the massage guidance matters less as children age ([bedtime-stories.fun](https://www.bedtime-stories.fun/blog/best-bedtime-story-apps)), which builds churn into the product's core differentiator. A rough implied figure (an inference) is about 9K paying subscribers against 1.5M installs, roughly **0.6% of lifetime installs paying**, at about $44 ARPU ([Alts.co](https://alts.co/should-you-invest-in-storybook-a-sleep-app-for-parents/)).

## Theo outsold Storybook within weeks of launch

The pivot started as one of many experiments. Bible stories released inside Storybook outperformed, so the team gave them their own app. "It only took a few weeks for Theo to surpass Storybook's daily sales," Cornejo said, and he linked prayer to bedtime because "sleep is universal and prayer was present in most of the family's routines" ([TWiST 2214](https://www.thisweekinstartups.com/2214)). Theo launched around March 2025 (App Store id6740779207, Android com.theopray). It is audio-first in English and Spanish, with Bible stories, guided prayers and devotionals built into a bedtime routine of about 9 minutes, priced at $49.99/yr ([faith.tools](https://faith.tools/app/theo); [AppAdvice](https://appadvice.com/app/theo-prayer-meditation/6740779207)). The company claims **400,000 families in 10 weeks** and **2M+ users** by late 2025 ([EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/816903255/meet-theo-the-first-prayer-meditation-app-for-kids-reaches-400-000-families-in-just-10-weeks); [familify.com](https://familify.com/)). Independent data is lower but still strong: about **740K Android downloads** by June 2026, a 1M+ milestone reported in September 2025, and 500K+ Play installs ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/theo-prayer-meditation/com.theopray); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/theo-prayer-meditation/6740779207)). A marketing case study claims "$200K/month in just 2 months," which is unverified ([Growth Hacking Lab](https://thegrowthhackinglab.com/case-studies/theo-200k-revenue-2-months/)). Familify now presents itself as the company behind Theo and Storybook, and most of the team works on Theo ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fxcornejo/)). The pivot shows that the same audio-bedtime-ritual engine sold far better when it was tied to an identity-laden need, faith, than to generic "sleep."

## Users praise the bonding but call the billing a trap

Because Reddit could not be read, the user-voice picture comes from app-store and review-site snippets. **No Reddit thread could be read directly.** One competitor-authored roundup claims that "multiple parents on Reddit" report unexpected renewals from Storybook and Moshi, citing tight cancellation windows, but it links no threads ([bedtime-stories.fun](https://www.bedtime-stories.fun/blog/best-bedtime-story-apps)). Searches turned up no organic Reddit, BabyCenter or What to Expect discussion of Storybook. That absence fits an app acquired through ads rather than recommended among parents, though it is evidence of absence, not a measurement.

The reviews themselves split cleanly. Positive reviews praise bonding and the bedtime routine and call the price modest for what it offers ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/storybook-calm-bedtime-sleep/id1355190474)). Negative reviews concentrate on billing. One Google Play reviewer says in-app cancellation "kept saying technical error" for 7 days before an annual charge landed ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.storybook.story)). Others report charges "wayyy before the free trial ended" and warn that the in-app unsubscribe "doesn't actually unsubscribe you" ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1355190474/storybook-sleep-massage-app/reviews)). Another says renewal ran by the hour rather than the day ([Google Play IN](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.storybook.story&hl=en_IN)), and one calls the app "definitely a scam" over price creep ([justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1355190474/storybook-sleep-massage-app/contact)).

The developer answers publicly, sending users to the Play Store to cancel and offering quick refunds at info@familify.com, and some reviewers say support was "very helpful" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.storybook.story&hl=en_US)). That refund offer contradicts the published no-refund terms. Trustpilot has only 2 reviews ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/storybook-app.com)). Comparison content is almost entirely written by competitors. It positions Moshi as the established library, Calm Kids as "kids content buried inside an adult app," and Hatch as tied to hardware, and none of them offers guided touch ([snoozly.io](https://snoozly.io/best-sleep-app-for-kids/); [sleepytale.com](https://www.sleepytale.com/compare/best-lullaby-app)).

## Lessons to copy and gaps to exploit for an eczema bedtime app

The evidence supports the core mechanic. A 2023 RCT of a bedtime massage intervention found improvements in infant sleep latency, night wakings and longest continuous sleep ([PubMed 37248915](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37248915/)), although a Cochrane review gives infant massage only "tentative support" ([Parent.com](https://www.parent.com/blogs/conversations/2025-comforting-touch-the-role-of-infant-massage-in-promoting-sleep)). Eczema-specific guidance makes the moisturizer step a natural anchor. It advises moisturizing within about 3 minutes of leaving the bath ([Every Thing For Dads](https://www.everythingfordads.com/dadsblog/how-dads-can-take-over-the-eczema-bedtime-routine)) and notes that night itch worsens as children "get hot under the blankets" ([Happy Skin](https://www.happy-skin.com/news/how-to-stop-my-toddler-or-child-itching-at-night-a-guide-for-parents/)). It also finds that "distraction is often more effective than talking about the itch" ([Capital Area Pediatrics](https://www.capitalareapediatrics.com/blog/how-to-soothe-eczema-itching-expert-advice-for-parents)), which is exactly what a story told during the moisturizer massage provides. Guidance conflicts on thick balms versus overheating ([RCH Melbourne](https://www.rch.org.au/uploadedFiles/Main/Content/derm/knowing-your-childs-eczema-booklet(1).pdf)), so content should defer to the child's clinician rather than prescribe products. The table below turns the Storybook record into specific moves. The recommendations are this report's analysis, built on the evidence cited above.

## Conclusion

Storybook shows that a strong, tactile, screen-free ritual can win awards, featuring and millions of downloads without becoming a growing business. Its weak point was positioning and trust, not the product mechanic. Generic sleep positioning forced the company into paid Meta acquisition, and aggressive annual billing kept the word of mouth that would have cut those costs from forming. The Theo result is the most useful finding for an eczema app: the same audio bedtime engine outsold the original within weeks once it was tied to a need parents feel as part of who they are. Eczema has the same features: an acute, nightly, named pain with active communities and clinicians who act as gatekeepers. That gives the new app a chance to start where Storybook ended up, as a niche ritual with honest billing and organic trust, instead of spending years on paid installs to learn it.
