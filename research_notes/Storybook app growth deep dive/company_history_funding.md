## 1. Origin story: founders, infant massage, the viral post, MVP

### Takeaway
Two Ecuadorians, Daniela Vega and her husband Francisco Cornejo, moved to Australia in early 2015 with sons aged 1 and 3 who fought bedtime. Vega trained in infant massage there. She struggled to massage and read a story at the same time, and that gap became an app combining massage, narration and music. The "1M+ views / 33,000 comments" viral story appears only in a company-distributed press release (OpenPR, around March 2021). That release says the story was used to launch the MVP. No source I found names the platform, the date, or what the post said.

### Cited Findings

Florida Trend lists Vega as **COO**, both aged 35 at the time; other sources call her co-founder and content or creative lead. — [Florida Trend](https://www.floridatrend.com/article/34022/husband-and-wife-team-launch-storybook-app-to-help-parents-connect-with-kids/)

## 4. The pivot to Theo and Familify's status in 2026

### Takeaway
After testing Bible stories inside Storybook, where faith content outperformed, Familify launched **Theo: Prayer & Meditation** (App Store id6740779207, Android com.theopray) in early 2025. Within weeks of launch, Theo's daily sales passed Storybook's. The company claims 400K families in 10 weeks (May 2025) and 2M+ users by late 2025 or 2026. Most of the team now works on Theo, and Storybook continues as the legacy app. Familify (Miami, 11–50 staff on LinkedIn) now presents itself as "the company behind Theo App and Storybook."

### Cited Findings
- **TWiST E2214, "Even great startups sometimes need to pivot (feat. Francisco Cornejo of Theo)," released around Nov 24 2025.** — [TWiST 2214](https://www.thisweekinstartups.com/2214); [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/7DekvahApfBrwfDEEk2uYu)
  - The storytelling app was "well reviewed but wasn't growing."
  - The team noticed Bible stories and faith content were especially popular, and launched Bible-based stories inside Storybook "as one experiment among many."
  - "It only took a few weeks for Theo to surpass Storybook's daily sales."
  - On why prayer: there was always a relationship between prayer and sleep — "sleep is universal and prayer was present in most of the family's routines."
  - Cornejo credits experimentation.

## 5. Founder interviews and stated growth lessons

### Takeaway
Cornejo's recurring themes: (1) aim at parents, not kids, and make the product a bonding ritual with no child screen time; (2) the founding story and Vega's expertise drive distribution; (3) Miami and the Latino-founder ecosystem helped with grants and investors; (4) run many experiments, follow what users love even if it means a new app (Theo); (5) faith and sleep share a ritual.

### Cited Findings
- **Design for parents.** Instead of designing an app for children, the product guides parents through massages while stories and relaxing sounds play. It is positioned as "without screen time" for children. — [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/francisco-cornejo:-promoting-parent-child-sleep-and-wellness-through-stories); [EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/553705841/storybook-app-helps-children-and-parents-sleep-better-without-screen-time)
- **Sleep problems are solvable.** Cornejo argues many childhood sleep problems "stem from causes that can easily be solved without medical treatment." — [Kingscrowd founder profile](https://kingscrowd.com/founder-profile-storybook-francisco-cornejo/)
